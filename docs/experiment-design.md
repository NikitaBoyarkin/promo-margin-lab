## Question

Does capping promo depth at the category breakeven (W3) improve incremental
contribution margin, without losing more revenue than the category owner accepts?

## Unit and arms

| | |
|---|---|
| Unit of randomisation | **store** (cluster), not store-week |
| Arms | 2: capped depth vs current practice |
| Stores | 120 total, 60 per arm |
| Duration | 8 weeks, no washout inside the window |
| Assignment | permute **within** each format stratum (convenience 62, supermarket 46, hyper 12 → 31/31, 23/23, 6/6) |
| Assignment artifact | one `store_id → arm` table, written once and reused by every replication and by the analysis |
| Primary metric | incremental contribution margin, ₽ per store-week, on **net units** (see `metric-definitions.md`) |
| Secondary | revenue, units, share of unprofitable waves (wave = store-week-category) |
| Guardrails | revenue decline > 2% vs control for 2 consecutive weeks; cannibalization ratio > 35% |

Cluster randomisation is required: the intervention is a category-level decision,
so stores are the smallest independent unit and store-week observations are not
independent.

**Stratification is a requirement, not a description.** The simulation in
`src/experiment.py:109` currently permutes all 120 stores flat, so the assigned
design has never been the one this table describes. PENDING.

## Exposure inside the window

The 8-week window is not 8 promo weeks. On the current panel the promo share by
week is:

| Week | 70 | 71 | 72 | 73 | 74 | 75 | 76 | 77 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `promo_flag` | 14.1% | 14.7% | 0% | 0% | 12.6% | 12.8% | 0% | 0% |

Promo runs in 4 of 8 weeks, and two of six categories (grocery, drinks) are not
promoted in the window at all. A per-store-week mean therefore dilutes the effect
by half: **MDE 786 ₽ per store-week is 1 572 ₽ per exposed store-week.** Any
comparison against the average promo loss must use the same exposure grain.

## Power

| | |
|---|---|
| ICC (between-store share of variance in incremental CM deviation) | **0.2197** |
| Between-store SD | 1 168.59 ₽ |
| Residual SD (weekly) | 2 154.04 ₽ |
| SD of store means over 8 weeks (σ_store) | 1 537.0 ₽ |
| MDE at α = 0.05, power = 0.80 | **786 ₽ / store-week** |
| Same MDE as % of weekly revenue per store (213 928 ₽) | **0.367%** |

The ICC above is computed on all 78 weeks, as `run()` does. The ICC restricted to
the 8-week experiment window is **0.3572**; power and MDE use window means, so the
window ICC is the relevant one to quote next to them.

The variance in the headline metric is the variance of **incremental** CM
(`dev_profit` = realised CM − baseline CM), not of raw CM. Raw CM is dominated by
store size (between-store SD 27 046 ₽, raw ICC 0.95–0.99) and cannot be powered at
any realistic store count.

4-week window.

### Two corrections to the reported power

- **77.8% is not a shortfall.** At 1 000 replications the binomial SE is 1.31 pp;
  77.8% is 1.7 SE below 80% (p ≈ 0.09). The analytic power with a t-distribution
  instead of z is **79.4%**; the gap is noise plus the z→t substitution, not a
  design fault. Report it as 77.8% ± 1.3 pp.
- **The metric base is gross, not net.** `src/experiment.py:43` uses
  `units_sold`; `metric-definitions.md` defines CM on `net_units`. On the current
  panel the gross base overstates incremental CM by 3.51 mn ₽ (17.2%). PENDING.

## Analysis plan

1. **SRM** — χ² of store counts against 50/50, threshold p < 0.001. Stop if it
   fires. Check **per stratum** as well: a global 60/60 can hide a 9/3 split in a
   12-store stratum.
2. **Primary** — Welch t-test on store means over the 8 weeks (cluster-robust by
   construction), two-sided α = 0.05. Report the effect in ₽ with a 95% CI and
   the number of analysable stores.
3. **Secondary** — same contrast on revenue and units, descriptive only, no
   multiplicity correction.
4. **Guardrails** — the statistic is defined in `metric-definitions.md`
   (one-sided z on the weekly contrast, threshold in SE units, α = 0.001 per
   read, Bonferroni over 8 weeks × 2 guardrails). The current 2% point-estimate
   rule is not a test: it fires under the null in 41.8% of 1 000 random splits
   and must be recalibrated to a ≤5% false-stop rate before it can be used.
5. **No peeking for the primary metric** — the test is read once, at 8 weeks.
6. **Optional stopping.** If a guardrail stops the test at week *w*, the primary
   is **not** tested. Report the CM contrast over *w* weeks as descriptive, with a
   sequential-adjusted CI (Lan–DeMets), or not at all. A guardrail stop selects on
   revenue, which is driven by the same promo intensity as the primary metric, so
   the naive CM estimate at the stop week is biased.
7. **Attrition.** An analysable store is one with at least 6 of 8 weeks observed.
   Stores below that bar are excluded before the test, never silently turned into
   NaN (a NaN currently passes `ttest_ind` and is counted as "not rejected").
   Minimum 45 analysable stores per arm (25% attrition).
8. **Estimand.** Intention-to-treat: the assignment effect. A `cap_applied` flag
   per store-week records compliance; the contamination rate is reported.
   CACE/LATE is secondary only, never the headline.
9. **Covariate adjustment** — not pre-registered. ANCOVA on pre-period store CM
   is a PENDING improvement; its MDE reduction has not been measured.

## Simulation validation

1 000 random splits of the 120 stores, outcome = store mean over the last 8 weeks:

| Scenario | Result |
|---|---|
| Null (no effect) rejection rate | **4.3%** (target 5%) |
| Power at the MDE (786 ₽) | **77.8%** (analytic 79.4%) |
| CI coverage of the true effect | **95.3%** |
| Mean estimate at the null | −11.77 ₽ |

A deliberately unbalanced split (57/63) is **not** flagged by SRM (p = 0.58); a
broken split (30/90) is (p < 1e-7). That is the intended sensitivity.

The guardrails, the secondary contrasts and attrition are **not** simulated by
`run()`. The "everything is validated by simulation" claim of v1 does not hold;
only the primary contrast and SRM are.

## Switchback

The switchback runs on **76 weeks**, against 8 for the cluster RCT:

| Scenario | Result |
|---|---|
| Null rejection rate | 5.2% |
| Rejection rate at an injected 786 ₽ | 98.6% |
| Injected carryover 0.5 recovered as | **0.495** |

The 98.6% is not comparable to the RCT's 77.8%: switchback uses store-weeks over
76 weeks, RCT uses store means over 8. Most of the gap is window length, not the
estimator. Before the switchback can be called more powerful, it must be re-run on
the same 8-week window. Its null rate (5.2%) is also above target and should be
checked at higher replication counts.

## What is frozen during the test

The treatment is a policy, so the inputs of the policy must not move while it is
being evaluated:

| Frozen | Why |
|---|---|
| Funding rate per SKU | A stepped funding rate moves the breakeven cap; the treatment would be redefined mid-test. The scenario is named in `decision-memo.md` but cannot occur in the current data — `funded_share` is constant per SKU |
| `list_price` per store-SKU | 24.5% of store-SKU change `list_price` inside the 8-week window on the current panel (mean step 2.4%, max 10.4%). Baseline and CM move with it |
| Category breakeven cap | Computed at week 0, held for the duration |

## Threats to validity (stated up front)

- **Spillover between formats** — a customer who switches store moves volume, not
  demand; the primary metric is margin, which still counts the sale, so the bias
  is small but non-zero.
- **Promo calendar** — the data has three mechanics (price cut, deal, multi-buy)
  and no national-campaign dimension. A national campaign cannot be withheld from
  either arm, so this is **not modelled**; in a real test it attenuates the
  contrast and must be logged as a covariate.
- **Seasonality** — the 8-week window sits on the seasonal peak (index 1.108 →
  1.129 across a 52-week cycle that ranges 0.865–1.129). Within-window drift is
  about ±2%; the result generalises to the peak, not to the year.
- **Compliance** — a store that does not apply the cap is effectively control;
  the analysis is intention-to-treat and reports the contamination rate.
- **Synthetic data** — the DGP has no competitor reaction and no weather. A real
  test would add both as covariates, and the MDE would rise.
- **Effect heterogeneity** — the simulation injects a constant ₽ effect, while
  the DGP's store size multiplier (0.6–1.8) and responsiveness imply a
  proportional effect. Power under a size-proportional effect is not measured.

## Roles

| Role | Holds |
|---|---|
| Decision owner | accepts or rejects the cap policy |
| Primary reader | reads the primary test at week 8, once |
| Stop authority | may stop on a guardrail breach or SRM |
| Escalation | category owner, for the revenue guardrail |

## What would make us stop

- SRM p < 0.001 at any read, overall or in any stratum.
- Revenue guardrail breached for two consecutive weeks, under the recalibrated
  statistic (not the current 2% point rule).
- Cannibalization ratio above 35% — the cap is supposed to lower it.
- Fewer than 45 analysable stores per arm (attrition > 25%).
